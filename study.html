Constitutional Law 13-Judge Bench · 1973

Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala

(1973) 4 SCC 225 · Supreme Court of India
Core Ratio Decidendi

"Parliament possesses wide constituent power to amend any part of the Constitution under Article 368, but this power does not extend to altering or destroying its Basic Structure."

  • Overruled Golak Nath (1967) while introducing the non-negotiable Basic Structure limitation.
  • Identified supremacy of Constitution, secularism, democracy, federalism, and judicial review as core unamendable pillars.
  • Preserved the harmonious balance between Fundamental Rights (Part III) and Directive Principles (Part IV).

Constitutional Law Cognitive Mnemonic · Art 19(1)

Article 19(1) Freedoms: The SAMROP Formula

Formula S — A — M — R — O — P
  • S: Speech and expression (Clause 19(1)(a))
  • A: Assemble peaceably without arms (Clause 19(1)(b))
  • M: Move freely throughout India (Clause 19(1)(d))
  • R: Reside and settle in any part of India (Clause 19(1)(e))
  • O: Organization & associations / unions (Clause 19(1)(c))
  • P: Profession, trade or business (Clause 19(1)(g))
💡 Memory Tip: Notice that sub-clause (1)(f) [Right to Property] was omitted by the 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978). Use SAMROP to never mix up clause letters in exams and pleadings!

Constitutional Law Cognitive Mnemonic · Art 19(2)

Article 19(2) Restrictions: The SOUP DISC Formula

Formula SOUP — DISC

Criminal Law & BNSS Decision Tree · Procedural Law

Cognizable vs Non-Cognizable: The WIB Decision Rule

Formula W — I — B (Warrant, Investigate, Bail)
  • W (Warrant): Cognizable = Police can arrest WITHOUT warrant; Non-Cognizable = Police REQUIRES warrant.
  • I (Investigation): Cognizable = Police must register FIR & investigate WITHOUT Magistrate order; Non-Cognizable = Requires Magistrate sanction under Sec 174 BNSS.
  • B (Bailability): Cognizable offenses are typically non-bailable; Non-cognizable offenses are almost universally bailable as a matter of right.
💡 Quick Exam Rule: Remember "Cognizable = Serious = Immediate Police Action"; "Non-Cognizable = Magistrate Permission First".

Corporate & SEBI Cognitive Mnemonic · SEBI LODR

SEBI LODR Committees: The ANSR Formula

Formula A — N — S — R ("ANSWER")
  • A: Audit Committee (Reg 18): Min 3 directors; 2/3rd must be Independent; Chairman must be Independent.
  • N: Nomination & Remuneration (Reg 19): Min 3 directors; all Non-Executive; 2/3rd Independent.
  • S: Stakeholders Relationship (Reg 20): Min 3 directors with at least 1 Independent Director.
  • R: Risk Management (Reg 21): Mandatory for top 1000 listed entities; majority Board members.
💡 Board Tip: The Chairman of the company can never chair the Audit Committee! Remember that both A and N require the strict 2/3rd Independent quorum.

Contracts & Civil 4-Step Legal Test · Section 56

Doctrine of Frustration: The 4-Step Legal Test

Standard Valid → Subsequent → Uncontrolled → Untenable
  • Step 1 (Valid Agreement): A subsisting, enforceable contract existed between the parties.
  • Step 2 (Subsequent Event): An unforeseen event occurred after the formation of the agreement.
  • Step 3 (Beyond Contemplation): The intervening event was beyond the control or fault of either party.
  • Step 4 (Basis Destroyed): Performance has become physically or legally impossible, not merely commercially difficult.
💡 Satyabrata Ghose Rule: Commercial hardship, price rise, or onerous execution does NOT constitute frustration under Section 56!
